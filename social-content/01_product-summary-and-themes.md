## Audience

Beginner and early-stage freelance video editors. They can edit (cuts, color,
captions), but they don't have a repeatable way to find and win clients. The
ads target English-speaking ages 18–44 with an interest in editing.

## Problems it solves (the five questions from the ads)

| The editor's question | Where the product answers it |
|---|---|
| What do I offer? | Offer Builder |
| Who do I contact? | 30-Day Plan + Prospect Tracker |
| What do I say? | Outreach Script Library |
| When do I follow up? | Prospect Tracker · Follow-Up Queue |
| What do I charge? | Pricing System |

Plus the later steps people often don't plan for: turning a reply into a call,
writing a proposal, onboarding, delivery and repeat work.

Core message: **"You can edit. Getting the client is a different skill, and
it's a sequence, not one trick."**

## Voice and guardrails

- Calm, practical, specific. Short sentences. No hype, no "six figures", no
  income screenshots, no "guaranteed".
- Talk to the reader as "you". Write as the LumiGoods brand, not as a
  personal editor story, until you confirm who is behind the account (see flags).
- Use the product's own honesty lines: "It won't contact anyone for you."
  "A structured routine you can run, measure and improve."
- Never present the tracker's demo rows (e.g. "Harbor Light Bakery (DEMO)") as
  real clients.
- Don't post full worksheets, full scripts, or full page screenshots. Use
  headings, one-line excerpts, or blurred pages (as the Demo ad does).
- CTA style: "If you want the full worksheets, CLIENT MACHINE is linked in
  bio." Never "BUY NOW".
