## Day 1 · Carousel · "Getting clients is a sequence"

**Goal:** saves and shares. No product mention.

**Slides (4:5)**
1. **Cover:** "Getting clients isn't one skill. It's a sequence." Sub: *10 steps most new editors skip around in*
2. **1 · Offer:** Decide what you sell and who it's for. *"I edit videos" is a skill, not an offer.*
3. **2 · Price:** Set a starting price before anyone asks. *Deciding on the spot is how you undercharge.*
4. **3 · Proof:** Show 2–3 examples that match the offer. *Not your whole hard drive.*
5. **4 · Prospects:** Make a list of real people or businesses who fit. *Names, not "YouTubers".*
6. **5 · Outreach:** Message them about something you can actually see. *Cold DMs are step 5, not step 1.*
7. **6 · Follow-up:** Most conversations need more than one message. *Write down when you'll follow up.*
8. **7–8 · Conversation → Proposal:** Turn interest into a clear next step, then put scope, price and timing in writing.
9. **9–10 · Onboarding → Delivery:** Start every project the same way. Deliver in a way that makes the second project easy.
10. **End:** "Which step do you usually skip? Comment the number." + save icon

**Caption**
> "Just send cold DMs" is the most common advice new video editors get.
>
> But outreach is step 5. If steps 1–4 aren't done, you're messaging people without a clear offer, a price or relevant examples. That's why replies feel random.
>
> The full sequence:
> Offer → Price → Proof → Prospects → Outreach → Follow-up → Conversation → Proposal → Onboarding → Delivery
>
> You don't need all ten done perfectly this week. Find the earliest step you've skipped and start there.
>
> Which number are you on right now? 👇
>
> Save this for your next planning session.

## Day 2 · Reel · "Stop asking 'Do you need an editor?'"

**Goal:** reach + comments. No product mention.
**Length:** ~25 s · 9:16 · text-on-screen, with optional voiceover

| Time | On screen | Voiceover / text |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2 s | Big text: **"Do you need an editor?"** with a red strike-through | "Stop sending this message." |
| 2–6 s | Phone mock: the DM, "Seen", no reply | "It makes them do all the thinking. They have to work out why they'd need you." |
| 6–12 s | Text: **"Point to something you can actually see."** Three cards fly in: *Long videos with no short clips · Old episodes not reused · Inconsistent captions* | "Before you message, look at their content. Find something specific you can genuinely observe. Not a problem you made up." |
| 12–20 s | Rewritten DM on screen (below) | "Then say what you noticed, offer one small idea, and ask an easy question." |
| 20–25 s | Text: **"Observe → suggest → ask."** + "Save for your next outreach session" | "Observe, suggest, ask." |

**Example DM on screen (write your own, this is a pattern, not a script from the paid library):**
> Hi Sam, I watched your latest podcast episode on pricing. The part at 14:20 about raising rates would work well as a 30-second vertical clip. I put together a quick idea of how I'd cut it. Want me to send it over?

**Caption**
> The problem with "Do you need an editor?" is that it asks them to diagnose themselves.
>
> A stronger first message points to something you can genuinely see in their content:
> → long videos that never get cut into short clips
> → great old episodes that never get reused
> → captions that change style from video to video
>
> Then: say what you noticed, offer one small, specific idea, and ask an easy yes/no question.
>
> Don't invent a problem. If you can't see one, that person might not be the right prospect yet, and that's useful to know too.
>
> Try it on 3 prospects this week and tell me what changed.

## Day 4 · Carousel · "Write your offer in one sentence"

**Goal:** saves. No product mention.

**Slides**
1. **Cover:** "Your offer, in one sentence." Sub: *If you can't say it simply, prospects can't repeat it.*
2. **The problem:** "I'm a video editor, I can edit anything" → *skill, not offer*
3. **Fill in the blanks:** "I help **[who]** turn **[what they already have]** into **[what they get]**, delivered **[how often / how fast]**."
4. **Example: podcasters.** "I help podcasters turn each full episode into 5 short vertical clips, delivered within 3 days of recording."
5. **Example: coaches.** "I help coaches turn their weekly live sessions into edited YouTube videos with captions, ready to post every Monday."
6. **Example: local businesses.** "I help local gyms turn phone footage into 4 short promo videos a month."
7. **Check it:** ✅ a specific who ✅ something they already have ✅ a clear deliverable ✅ a number or timeframe → *"Write yours in the comments."*

**Caption**
> Here's a quick test for your offer: could a prospect repeat it back to a friend after reading it once?
>
> A simple formula:
> I help [who] turn [what they already have] into [what they get], delivered [how often / how fast].
>
> Why "what they already have" matters: it shows you're working with something they already produce, not asking them to start from scratch.
>
> The examples in the slides are only starting points. Swap in your client type and the service you're best at.
>
> Drop your one-sentence offer below. Short and specific beats clever.

## Day 5 · Reel · "The 60-second portfolio audit"

**Length:** ~30 s · 9:16 · fast checklist reveal with screen recording of a portfolio page (use your own or a mock, not a real client's without permission)

| Time | On screen | Voiceover / text |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2 s | **"Audit your portfolio in 60 seconds"** + timer | "Open your portfolio. Let's audit it." |
| 2–8 s | ✅ **1. First thing they see = your best piece** | "Is the first video your best one, and does it match the client you want?" |
| 8–13 s | ✅ **2. Matches your offer** | "Selling short clips? Show short clips, not a wedding film from two years ago." |
| 13–18 s | ✅ **3. 2–3 pieces, not 20** | "Two or three strong, relevant pieces beat a long list." |
| 18–23 s | ✅ **4. One line of context per piece** | "Who was it for, what did you do, what was the goal?" |
| 23–27 s | ✅ **5. Obvious next step** | "Can they contact you in one tap?" |
| 27–30 s | **"No client work yet? Make a spec piece for your target client."** | "No client work yet? Make one sample for the client you want." |

**Caption**
> A portfolio doesn't have to be big. It has to be relevant.
>
> 60-second audit:
> 1. The first thing they see is your best piece, and it matches your target client
> 2. The examples match the offer you're selling
> 3. 2–3 strong pieces instead of everything you've ever made
> 4. One line of context per piece (for whom, what you did, the goal)
> 5. A clear, one-tap way to contact you
>
> No client work yet? Create a spec sample: take publicly available long-form content in your niche and show how you'd cut it. Label it clearly as a sample, and don't post someone else's footage publicly without permission.
>
> Which of the 5 did you fail? (Number 4 is the most common.)

Note to you: "Number 4 is the most common" is an opinion, not data. Cut it if you prefer.

## Day 6 · Checklist (3-slide carousel) · "Build a prospect tracker today"

**Goal:** saves + soft product mention.

**Slides**
1. **Cover:** "If it's not written down, you won't follow up." Sub: *Build a simple prospect tracker in 10 minutes*
2. **The 6 columns** (spreadsheet look):
   | Prospect / Business | Contact name | Platform | Contact method | Last contact | Next step + date |
   Example row: *"[Podcast name] · Sam · YouTube · Email · 12 Oct · Follow up 16 Oct"*
3. **Rules:** ☐ Add 5 prospects before you send anything ☐ Fill "Next step" every time you message someone ☐ Check the "Next step" column every morning ☐ Never leave a row with no date

**Caption**
> Most follow-ups don't get missed because people are lazy. They get missed because nobody wrote them down.
>
> Open Google Sheets or Excel and make 6 columns:
> Prospect / Business · Contact name · Platform · Contact method · Last contact · Next step + date
>
> Two rules make it work:
> → Every time you send a message, fill in the next step and a date.
> → Every morning, sort by that date and do what's due.
>
> That's a working pipeline. No software needed.
