## About the product

**"What's included?"**
> 10 connected PDF workbooks (192 pages), a day-by-day 30-day plan, an outreach and follow-up script library, a Prospect Tracker spreadsheet (.xlsx) with a dashboard and follow-up queue, and 2 editable Canva templates (a proposal and a client onboarding sheet). It's one ZIP, $47 USD one-time. Link in bio.

**"Will this get me clients?"**
> We can't promise that, and we'd be wary of anyone who does. CLIENT MACHINE gives you a structured routine: what to offer, who to contact, what to say, when to follow up and what to charge. It won't contact anyone for you. What you get out of it depends on running the process consistently.

**"Is this for experienced editors?"**
> It's built for beginner and early-stage freelance editors, people who can edit but don't yet have a repeatable way to find and win clients. If you already have a steady pipeline, it may be more structure than you need.

**"Do I need Canva / Excel?"**
> The Canva templates need a free Canva account. The Prospect Tracker is an .xlsx spreadsheet. Everything else is PDF.

**"Does it tell me what to charge?"**
> Not a number. Rates depend on your scope, costs and experience. The Pricing System walks you through choosing a pricing model, estimating your own floor, and setting a starting price or range you can say out loud.

**"Is it AI-generated?" / "Does it use AI?"**
> Some modules include optional AI drafting workflows that work with any general-purpose AI tool. The rule throughout is that AI creates a draft and you check it and decide. Nothing gets sent without your review.

## Engagement replies (organic posts)

**Someone shares their one-sentence offer (Day 4):**
> Nice. This is clear. One idea: make the [who / deliverable / timeframe] a bit more specific, e.g. "[suggestion]". Could someone repeat it back after reading it once?

**"Isn't following up annoying?" (Day 9):**
> It can be if every message repeats the last one. Keep nudges short, stop when someone says no, and only write again when there's something genuinely new.

**"How much should I charge?"**
> Hard to say without knowing the scope: how much footage, how long the final video is, the turnaround and the revisions all change the answer. Start by defining that, then set a starting price for it. Our Day 8 post has the common mistakes to avoid.

## Note on the refund wording

Your policy opens with "No refunds allowed" and then lists two cases where you
*do* refund. In public replies the drafts say "no change-of-mind refunds" so the
two parts don't contradict each other. Consider using the same wording on the
Gumroad page. Consumer rules for digital downloads differ by country (for example
in the UK and EU), so check the policy covers the countries you sell to. This is
not legal advice.
