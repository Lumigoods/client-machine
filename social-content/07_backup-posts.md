## Backup A · Carousel · "Revision, scope change or correction?"

**Source idea:** Pricing System, "Revisions without scope creep". The three
categories are named there. The examples below are new.

**Slides**
1. **Cover:** "'Just one small change…' Which kind is it?"
2. **Revision** = an adjustment *inside* what you agreed. *e.g. "Can the intro be a bit faster?"*
3. **Scope change** = it changes the agreement itself. *e.g. "Can you also make a vertical version for TikTok?"*
4. **Correction** = it doesn't match what was agreed. *e.g. the logo you were sent is the wrong one in the final cut.* If the mistake is yours, fix it. That's not an extra.
5. **The test:** "Would this have changed my quote if they'd asked at the start?" Yes → probably a scope change.
6. **What to do with a scope change:** pause → name what changed → decide whether scope or price needs to change → confirm with the client *before* doing it.
7. **End:** "Agree what one revision round means before the project starts."

**Caption**
> Most scope creep doesn't start with a big request. It starts with "just one small change."
>
> Naming the kind of change tells you what to do:
> 🔁 Revision: inside the agreed work. Do it.
> ➕ Scope change: changes the agreement. Pause and confirm scope or price first.
> 🛠 Correction: doesn't match the brief. If it's your mistake, fix it, no charge.
>
> Quick test: would it have changed your quote if they'd asked at the start?
>
> Save this for your next "quick tweak" message.

## Backup B · Reel · "'Can you do a free sample?'"

**Source idea:** Outreach Script Library, Section 5. You don't automatically owe
unpaid custom work, and there are three legitimate options.

| Time | On screen | Voiceover / text |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3 s | **"Can you do a free test edit?"** | "A prospect asks for a free sample. What do you say?" |
| 3–7 s | **You don't automatically owe unpaid work.** | "First: you don't automatically owe unpaid custom work." |
| 7–13 s | **Option A: show existing work** that proves the same skill | "Option one: send existing work that shows the same skill." |
| 13–19 s | **Option B: a very small sample** with the scope defined *before* you agree | "Option two: if it makes sense, a very small sample. Define exactly how small first." |
| 19–25 s | **Option C: a small paid trial** | "Option three: suggest a small paid test project." |
| 25–30 s | **"Choose on purpose, not by default."** | "None of these is wrong. Just choose on purpose." |

**Caption**
> "Can you do a free sample first?" is a normal question. You still get to decide how to answer it.
>
> Three fair options:
> A — share existing work that shows the skill they need
> B — offer a very small sample, and agree the exact scope before you start
> C — suggest a small paid trial project
>
> What you want to avoid is a full free edit with no scope that turns into three free edits.
>
> Which one do you usually go with?

## Backup C · Carousel · "Before you hit send"

**Source idea:** Outreach Script Library, personalization check + Message Quality
Scorecard. This is a shortened checklist in new words, not the full scorecard.

**Slides**
1. **Cover:** "Before you hit send: a 5-point check"
2. **Right name, right channel/business.** Copy-paste mistakes end conversations.
3. **One real, specific detail.** Something you actually saw, not "love your content!"
4. **Nothing invented.** No made-up compliments, stats or claims.
5. **One clear next step.** Not three questions and a portfolio link.
6. **Sounds like a person.** Read it out loud.
7. **End:** "If it fails one, it's not ready yet." + save icon

**Caption**
> 30 seconds of checking can save a first impression.
>
> Before sending any outreach message:
> ☐ correct name and business or channel
> ☐ one real detail you actually observed
> ☐ no invented compliments, facts or claims
> ☐ one clear, simple next step
> ☐ it sounds like you, not a template
>
> If it fails one, fix it before you send. Save this one.

## Backup D · Single image · "How to answer 'How much?'"

**Source idea:** Pricing System, "Say your price clearly" (price + scope + next
step) and the difference between a starting price, a range and a final quote.
The example lines are new, and none of the workbook's wording is used.

**Image (4:5):** Three stacked cards:
- **Price** → "It's $___ …"
- **Scope** → "…for [what it covers]."
- **Next step** → "If that works, I'll send over a short written scope."

Footer: *Starting price, range or final quote? Use the one that matches what you actually know.*

**Caption**
> When someone asks "How much?", a clear answer has three parts:
>
> 1. the price
> 2. what that price covers
> 3. what happens next
>
> And pick the right type of answer:
> → you know the full scope → give a final quote
> → one detail is still open → give a starting price for the base scope
> → the main variable is unknown → give a range and say what it depends on
>
> Then stop talking. No apologising, no instant discount.
